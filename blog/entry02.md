# Entry 2: Research & Synthesize
##### 11/16/26

## Content
After choosing playing the violin as my topic in Blog 1, I began exploring how technology is used in the music field, specifically for violinists. I wanted to find out what inventions already exist and how they help musicians with practicing, learning, and performing. I started with general research about violin technology, but I soon realized that there were many different types of innovations. To make my research more manageable, I explored software, physical gadgets, and more advanced technologies.

### Software & Applications
I first looked into software because I wanted to see how applications could help violinists improve without requiring additional physical equipment. I searched terms such as "violin practice apps" and "violin software." Two applications that I decided to investigate further were **Trebella** and **Metronaut.** [Trebella](https://trebel.la/) provides personalized practice plans and feedback to help violinists improve their playing. It uses a microphone and software to evaluate aspects of a musician's performance. [Metronaut Sheet Music](https://metronautapp.com/) takes a different approach by providing digital sheet music and musical accompaniments. It can also give feedback about pitch and rhythm, allowing musicians to work on their accuracy while practicing. Although these applications have different features, they both use digital technology to make practicing more interactive. I found it interesting that musicians can receive feedback through an application instead of depending entirely on someone else to identify mistakes.

### Hardware and Gadgets
After looking into software, I wanted to find out what kinds of physical devices had been developed specifically for violinists. I searched for "violin electronic gadgets" and "violin hardware." This led me to two inventions: **the Matter Squared ASR Smart Violin Shoulder Rest** and the **Xvive U9 Wireless Violin System.** [Matter Squared ASR: Smart Violin Shoulder Rest](https://mattersquared.com/products/asr-founders-edition) is a shoulder rest that uses vibrations to help violinists feel rhythm. I found this particularly interesting because it adds an electronic feature to an accessory that is normally used simply to support the violin. [Xvive U9 Wireless Violin System](https://xvive.com/audio/product/u9-violin-wireless-system/) has a different function. It uses a microphone and wireless transmitter to send sound to a receiver, allowing the violinist to connect to audio equipment without needing a long cable.
