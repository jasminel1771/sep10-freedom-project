# Entry 2: Research & Synthesize
##### 11/16/26

## Content
After choosing playing the violin as my topic in Blog 1, I began exploring how technology is used in the music field, specifically for violinists. I wanted to find out what inventions already exist and how they help musicians with practicing, learning, and performing. I started with general research about violin technology, but I soon realized that there were many different types of innovations. To make my research more manageable, I explored software, physical gadgets, and more advanced technologies.

#### Software & Applications
I first looked into software because I wanted to see how applications could help violinists improve without requiring additional physical equipment. I searched terms such as "violin practice apps" and "violin software." Two applications that I decided to investigate further were **Trebella** and **Metronaut.** [Trebella](https://trebel.la/) provides personalized practice plans and feedback to help violinists improve their playing. It uses a microphone and software to evaluate aspects of a musician's performance. [Metronaut Sheet Music](https://metronautapp.com/) takes a different approach by providing digital sheet music and musical accompaniments. It can also give feedback about pitch and rhythm, allowing musicians to work on their accuracy while practicing. Although these applications have different features, they both use digital technology to make practicing more interactive. I found it interesting that musicians can receive feedback through an application instead of depending entirely on someone else to identify mistakes.

#### Hardware & Gadgets
After looking into software, I wanted to find out what kinds of physical devices had been developed specifically for violinists. I searched for "violin electronic gadgets" and "violin hardware." This led me to two inventions: **the Matter Squared ASR Smart Violin Shoulder Rest** and the **Xvive U9 Wireless Violin System.** [Matter Squared ASR: Smart Violin Shoulder Rest](https://mattersquared.com/products/asr-founders-edition) is a shoulder rest that uses vibrations to help violinists feel rhythm. I found this particularly interesting because it adds an electronic feature to an accessory that is normally used simply to support the violin. [Xvive U9 Wireless Violin System](https://xvive.com/audio/product/u9-violin-wireless-system/) has a different function. It uses a microphone and wireless transmitter to send sound to a receiver, allowing the violinist to connect to audio equipment without needing a long cable. These two devices made me realize that even small accessories can be redesigned to include technology. Rather than changing how a violin produces sound, they focus on improving specific parts of practicing and performing. 

#### Advanced Violin Technology
For my final category, I wanted to investigate technologies that involved more complicated engineering and digital systems. I refined my searches to phrases such as "advanced violin technology" and "artificial intelligence in violin playing." Two examples I found were **VAIolin** and the **Glasser/Voltatron Electric Violin.** [VAIolin](https://www.cs.umd.edu/article/2024/04/umd-phd-student-snehesh-shresthas-software-uses-ai-teach-you-how-play-violin) was especially interesting because it combines music with artificial intelligence and computer vision. Developed through research at the **University of Maryland,** this system analyzes a violinist's posture, movements, and playing techniques. It uses this information to provide feedback that can help musicians understand how they perform. Unlike applications that mainly focus on the sound, VAIolin also examines the physical movements involved in playing.  [Glasser/Voltatron Electric Violin](https://electricviolinshop.com/products/volta-glassertron-glv-aex4-white-4-string-carbon-composite-hybrid-electric-violin-with-leds?srsltid=AYCX9vLS5yL94qqSHRG3lP_s8gHqfnHSj2N4bmRiRSebRyy5ZQ8iDbkB) demonstrates how electronic engineering can change the instrument itself. It uses electronic components to amplify sound and connect to external audio equipment. This gives musicians more possibilities when performing and experimenting with amplified music. These technologies introduced me to some of the more advanced ways engineering can be connected to music. I had initially thought mostly about applications and electronic instruments, but researching VAIolin helped me discover that artificial intelligence and computer vision can also be used to study musical performance. 

#### How I synthesized my Research
After collecting my examples, I had to decide how to organize them beyond simply separating software from hardware. I wanted my categories to explain the differences between the inventions rather than just describe what type of device they were. One organization method I considered was grouping everything by its purpose. For example, I could have separated the technologies into practice, performance, and technique improvement. However, I noticed that some inventions could belong to more than one group. Trebella, for instance, helps with practicing while also providing feedback about technique. This would make the categories overlap. Another possibility was organizing everything according to the technology each invention uses. I could have separated them into categories such as microphones, wireless communication, artificial intelligence, and electronic components. However, this would make it harder to compare inventions that use several different technologies at once. I eventually decided to arrange my research according to complexity. I chose this because the examples I found varied significantly in how they operated and the technology involved. It allowed me to move from applications with more straightforward functions to systems that combine multiple advanced technologies.

#### This is how I organized my findings:
**1. Least Complex: Violin Software & Apps**
* Trebella
* Metronaut Sheet Music

**2. Mildly Complex: Violin Hardware & Gadgets**
* Matter Squared ASR Smart Violin Shoulder Rest
* Xvive U9 Wireless Violin System
   
**3. Most Complex: Advanced Violin Technology**
* VAIolin
* Glasser/Voltatron Electric Violin

## Sources 
Findings the technologies was only one part of my research. I also needed to understand how each invention actually worked. I used product descriptions, informational websites, and research related sources to gather details about their functions and components. When I found an interesting invention, I searched for its name separately instead of relying only on the website when I first discovered it. This allowed me to find more detailed explanations and understand its purpose. I also paid attention to where the information came from, especially when researching more advanced technologies such as VAIolin.

## Skills

#### Organizing
One skill I practiced was organizing information in a clear and useful way. Since I found several different types of violin technology, I needed a system that would make my research easier to understand. I decided to separate the inventions into three categories based on their complexity: software and apps, hardware and gadgets, and advanced technology. I also kept track of the links and information connected to each invention so I could find them again when needed. This helped me avoid mixing up details and made it easier to compare the technologies. I learned that organizing research is not just about keep information together, but also about arranging it in a way that makes the connections between different topics easier to understand.
